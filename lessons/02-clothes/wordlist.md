## This / These — That / Those

This is singular and these are plural. They show that the things are close to the speaker.
That is singular and those are plural. They show that the things are far from the speaker.

- This is a hat. These are hats.
- This is a suit. These are suits.
- This is a coat. These are coats.
- This is a shirt. These are shirts.
- This is a belt. These are belts.
- This is a skirt. These are skirts.
- This is a dress. These are dresses.
- This is a blouse. These are blouses.
- This is a button. These are buttons.
- This is a sock. These are socks.
- That is a sweater. Those are sweaters.
- That is a watch. Those are watches.
- That is a ring. Those are rings.
- That is a mirror. Those are mirrors.
- That is a necklace. Those are necklaces.
- That is a pocket. Those are pockets.
- That is a tie. Those are ties.
- That is a bracelet. Those are bracelets.
- That is a glove. Those are gloves.
- That is an umbrella. Those are umbrellas.

## Questions and answers

- Is this a belt? — Yes, that is a belt. / No, that is not a belt.
- Is this a dress belt? — Yes, that is a dress belt.
- Is this a toothbrush? — No, that is not a toothbrush.
- Is this a new suit? — Yes, that is a new suit.
- Is this an old umbrella? — No, that is not an old umbrella.
- Is this a pocket mirror? — Yes, that is a pocket mirror.
- Is this an expensive ring? — No, that is not an expensive ring.
- Are these short pants? — Yes, those are short pants.
- Are these clean clothes? — No, those are not clean clothes.
- Are these long socks? — Yes, those are long socks.

## Adjectives — or

- Is this coat long or short? — That coat is long.
- Is this glove left or right? — That glove is right.
- Is this necklace old or new? — That necklace is old.
- Is this suit big or little? — That suit is big.
- Are these ties clean or dirty? — Those ties are clean.
- Are these clothes beautiful or not? — Those clothes are very beautiful.
- Are these glasses expensive or not? — Those glasses are very expensive.

## Possessive adjectives

my, your, his, her, its, our, their

- That is my gray coat.
- That is your green sweater.
- That is his black comb.
- That is her pink brush.
- That is its blue button.
- Our new umbrella is orange.
- Your shoe size is very big.
- Their little eyes are very sad.
- Those are my new shoes.
- Those are your blue socks.
- Those are his brown boots.
- Those are her green skirts.

## Possessive form of names

's at the end of a name may show possession.

- This is John's new suit.
- This is Mary's blue sweater.
- This is Eugene's gray hat.
- This is Helen's expensive ring.
- This is Paul's clean, white shirt.
- This is Edward's beautiful, old watch.
- Elizabeth's skirt is blue and gray.
- Andrew's tie is red and white.
- Ashley's suit is pink and yellow.
- Nathaniel's long socks are brown.
- Katherine's new blouse is green.

## Whose...?

- Is this my comb? — No, that is not your comb.
- Whose comb is this? — That is Andrew's pocket comb.
- Is this your beautiful, blue sweater? — No, that is not my size.
- Whose blue sweater is this? — That is Philip's blue sweater.
- Are these Matthew's big, brown boots? — No, those are not his big, brown boots.
- Whose big, brown boots are these? — Those are Nathaniel's big, brown boots.
- Whose clean clothes are these? — Those are Mrs. Kennedy's clean clothes.
- Whose beautiful, white gloves are these? — Those are Mrs. Kennedy's beautiful, white gloves.

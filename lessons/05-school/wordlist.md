## about / with / without / for / at

- What is this book about? — It is about language.
- What language is it about? — It is about the English language.
- What was your last class about yesterday? — It was about history.
- What history was it about? — It was about the history of China.
- Who was Lisa with? — Lisa was with her friends, Alexandra and Natalie.
- Is this coffee with or without sugar? — That coffee is without sugar.
- Was Jessica in New York with or without her friends? — She was there without her friends.
- Who is this ice cream for? — It is for Ann.
- Who are all these books for? — They are all for her friend, William.
- What is this board for? — It is for a new shelf.
- When is your English class? — It is at 9:15 every Monday, Wednesday, and Friday morning.
- Where was Mr. Johnson yesterday? — He was at the library. He is there every day.

## Prepositions and pronouns

about, at, between, of, for, on, off, to, from, in, out of, with, without

- me / us
- you / you
- him / them
- her
- it

- What was the chicken out of? — The chicken was out of the egg. Out of it? — Yes, out of it!
- Who was the new student between? — He was between John and Philip. Between them? — Yes, between them!
- What was Matthew on? — He was on your new coffee table. On it? — Yes, on it!
- Who are these papers for? — They are for Katherine, my sweetheart. For her? — Oh yes! For her!
- Who is this a picture of? — It is a picture of our first English class. Of us? — Yes, of us!
- Who is this history book from? — It is from me to you. From you to me? — Yes, from me to you, my old friend!

- Was there a map on the blackboard? — Yes, there was a map on the blackboard.

- Will there be more seats at all those tables tomorrow? — Yes, there will be ten or more seats at all those tables before 10 o'clock tomorrow.

## How many...? / How much...?

how many is used when a number can be put before a word, for example: student, teacher, table.

- all = 35
- many = 26, 27, 28
- some = 7, 8, 9
- few = 3, 4, 5
- no = 0

- How many lemons are yellow? — All lemons are yellow.
- How many shelves are there at the library? — There are many shelves at the library.
- How many problem words are there on the blackboard? — There are some problem words on the blackboard.
- How many students are with the teacher? — There are a few students with the teacher.
- How many pencils are there in my hand? — There are no pencils in my hand.

how much is used when a number can not be put before a word, for example: water, rice, salt.

- all = 35
- much = 26, 27, 28
- some = 7, 8, 9
- little = 3, 4, 5
- no = 0

- How much ice cream is cold? — All ice cream is cold.
- How much milk is there in his coffee? — There is much milk in his coffee.
- How much butter is there on his bread? — There is some butter on his bread.
- How much sugar is there in her tea? — There is little sugar in her tea.
- How much meat is there in the soup? — There is no meat in the soup.

## Some / Any

some is used in a positive sentence. any is used in a negative, or question sentence.

- There is some coffee. — There is not any coffee. — Is there any coffee?
- There is some rice. — There is not any rice. — Is there any rice?
- There is some soup. — There is not any soup. — Is there any soup?
- There is some more meat. — There is not any more meat. — Is there any more meat?
- There are some problems. — There are not any problems. — Are there any problems?
- There are some pens. — There are not any pens. — Are there any pens?
- There are some things. — There are not any things. — Are there any things?
- There are some more questions. — There are not any more questions. — Are there any more questions?

- Is there any apple pie? — Yes, there is some apple pie. / No, there is not any apple pie.
- Is there any sugar? / Is there any hot tea? / Is there any chocolate milk? / Is there any ice water? / Is there any white bread?
- Are there any library books? / Are there any good seats? / Are there any new pencils? / Are there any easy teachers? / Are there any difficult questions?

## it

it may be used in place of a known thing.

- What color is your test paper? — It is white, milk white!
- What color is Mary's hair? — It is brown, chocolate brown!
- What color is your clean sweater? — It is blue, ocean blue!
- Is my answer right or wrong? — Without question, it is right.
- Is the board long or short? — Without question, it is long, too long.
- Is her picture big or little? — Without question, it is big, too big.
- Is English easy or difficult? — Without question, it is easy with this book.

it is also used to show a general condition.

- Excuse me. What time is it? — It is 8:00. / It is 9:05. / It is 10:30.
- Is it hot or cold in Russia? — It is very cold, too cold for me.
- Is it expensive or not in Paris? — It is expensive, but not too expensive for you.
- Is it far from London to New York? — Yes, it is very far, too far for us.
- Is it far from Beijing to Shanghai? — No, it is close, but not too, too close.
